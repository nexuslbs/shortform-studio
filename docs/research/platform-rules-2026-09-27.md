### YouTube "inauthentic content" policy (spam / deceptive practices / repetitious content renamed)
*   **What exactly disqualifies templated or mass-produced AI content:** YouTube's policy aims to prevent "AI slop" – mass-produced, low-effort, or repetitive AI-generated content. Content is disqualified if it is spam, scams, or deceptive practices, especially when AI is used to mislead viewers. Monetization can be revoked for content without "original work or significant original commentary," meaning added value, modifications, or educational/entertainment elements beyond simple re-uploads or generic templates. Channels featuring AI-generated personas discussing sensitive topics like finance or health may also be ineligible for the YouTube Partner Program.

### YouTube synthetic/altered content disclosure
*   **When disclosure is REQUIRED for AI-generated video:** Disclosure is required when AI is used to meaningfully alter or generate realistic content that could mislead viewers (e.g., making a real person appear to say or do something they didn't, altering real event footage, generating a realistic fictional scene). It is not required for clearly unrealistic content, animations, special effects, or AI used for production assistance that doesn't create misleading realistic content.
    *   Source: YouTube AI Content Policy: Disclosure & Monetization - Layer3Labs: https://layer3labs.io/youtube-ai-content-policy/ (Accessed: 2026-09-27)
*   **Exact field/Studio setting used:** The "altered or synthetic content" flag in YouTube Studio. For API uploads, this corresponds to the boolean field `status.containsSyntheticMedia` in the YouTube Data API v3, set during `videos.insert` or `videos.update` calls.
    *   Source: Disclosing use of GenAI content - Android - YouTube Help: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13845309?hl=en (Accessed: 2026-09-27)
    *   Source: Videos | YouTube Data API - Google for Developers: https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFtEOHTuyVr8kI6_tTRZw0Xr2wt-hXFpqBqymkLFjbJKxJ3sWlbFkDV9MfR_bSWBOtc_qnUmhV4oaQW9G_2LukvFp1FBG-c0mZ_frIfTX4G7Oi3nPDM0CjZyXhPeANf3H-w-Gid5jbdyNwEZoeyCJc= (Accessed: 2026-09-27)

### Affiliate/FTC disclosure requirement wording
*   **General requirements:** Clear, conspicuous disclosure for any "material connection" (payments, free products, commissions). Use plain language ("advertisement," "ad," "sponsored"). Placement must be prominent and near the link/endorsement.
    *   Source: FTC Affiliate Disclosure - Termly: https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQGP8y7y2YqT0mjs_7rbio_45hDrUdd89u_IOelNOeK4RC_E1iaA6X0XyJfdUAdOlL02rHJTW36sfsVITLh0LLxvYEWq6B3NDJXfbLtjpM9XlEFr5rrbR5wnIGNaxIZtqmQQJY8xmYF29yyBSl19gp9dRGnGtpHznasT (Accessed: 2026-09-27)
*   **YouTube specific:** Check "paid promotion" box, verbally mention in video (ideally early), and include in description *above* "...more" button.
    *   Example: "This video contains paid promotion." or "I receive a commission from purchases made through the links in this description."
*   **Instagram specific (Reels/Stories):** "#ad" or "#affiliate" in first line of caption (before "more"). For Reels, text overlay within first 3 seconds. For Stories, visible for entire duration. Use "Paid Partnership" feature.
    *   Example: "#ad" or "Affiliate link in bio."
